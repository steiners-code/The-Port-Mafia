# LinkedIn Strategy

This is the strategic foundation behind everything you decide when working
on LinkedIn — what the platform is actually for, how a CTA earns its
place, and how hook and media function as one decision, not two. Absorb
it before deciding what to post or how to write it.

---

## 1. Goals

Every decision anywhere in this pipeline traces back to exactly one of
three goals. If a choice doesn't clearly serve one, it isn't ready to ship.

1. **Professional Portfolio** — showcase real projects and the journey
   behind them. Not a resume; a running account of what's actually being
   built.
2. **Connections & Community** — build a network of genuine value, not a
   follower count. The win condition is real conversation, not reach.
3. **Money** — freelance clients or job opportunities. The quiet goal:
   earning trust from people who will never comment, react, or repost.

---

## 2. The CTA Doctrine

A CTA always serves exactly one of three purposes:

- **Driving engagement** — repost, comment, follow.
- **Capturing an email.**
- **Opening a DM.**

**The underlying philosophy:** give real value for free — a genuine
technique, a genuine lesson, something useful entirely on its own merits.
But the deeper artifact behind it — the actual template, the actual guide,
the actual walkthrough — is never fully free. It costs the reader
_something_: their attention (engagement), their email, or a real
conversation (a DM). This isn't a trick. It's an honest exchange: free
insight up front, paid depth behind it.

**Which CTA type to use is chosen based on what actually exists to receive
it.** If there's no page or list currently set up to capture an email,
don't manufacture a CTA promising one — default to engagement or DM
instead. A CTA that leads somewhere that doesn't exist is worse than no
CTA at all.

---

## 3. Hook & Media Doctrine

Media and hook are not two separate decisions — they're the same decision
expressed in two formats, the way a video's thumbnail and title only work
as a pair. Media's job is to stop the scroll. Hook's job is to convert
that stopped attention into a click on "…see more." Neither compensates
for the other: strong media under a flat hook gets seen and scrolled past;
a strong hook under generic media doesn't get looked at long enough to be
read in the first place.

**LinkedIn's hook format is a hard platform constraint, not a style
preference:** one to two lines, at most a single line break, everything
above the "…see more" truncation. The hook has to independently create
curiosity within that space. It is not a teaser for an incomplete
sentence — it is a complete, self-contained pull.

Structural pattern (deliberately generic — see Section 5 on why):

```
[a plainly stated, specific claim or question]
[a one-line reframe, stance, or consequence]
```

---

## 4. Hook, Body, and CTA Are Non-Overlapping

These are three separate strings that concatenate, in order, into the
actual post. `body` picks up **after** `hook` ends — it does not restate
or re-open with the hook's line. `cta` is its own closing block — it does
not get folded into `body`'s final paragraph, and it is not the same
content as the first-comment.

This is a structural rule, not a suggestion: nothing in `hook` may
reappear verbatim inside `body`. Every persona that produces final post
content is bound by this; it is not optional per-persona style.

---

## 5. Examples Are Structural, Never Literal

Never anchor to something you could mimic verbatim. A specific example,
once given, tends to become the reproduced pattern rather than one option
among many — its topic, phrasing, even language can leak into unrelated
output. Treat every example you encounter — including the pattern in
Section 3 — as illustrating structure only, brackets and placeholders,
never real content to reproduce.
